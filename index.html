WHY THIS ARCHIVE?

Reading the texts that teach India to its children.

School textbooks do more than teach language, literature, history or social science. They also show students what kinds of people, lives, places and futures matter.

This archive brings together texts taught to students in Classes 8–10 across different Indian states, boards and languages, and asks what we can learn from the worlds these texts create.

What happens when a child sees themselves in what they read — and what happens when they don't?

ABOUT THE PROJECT

This is an archive — and a research project.

How will texts be studied?

Each text can be examined for its characters, settings, themes, identities, relationships, occupations, aspirations and forms of belonging.

Rather than assuming that a group is simply "absent", the project distinguishes between what is explicitly represented, what is underrepresented in the sampled material, and what requires further research.

  • Class and textbook
  • Board and state
  • Language and subject
  • Characters and identities
  • Themes and experiences
  • Representation and omission
  • Questions of identification
  • Possibilities and aspirations
